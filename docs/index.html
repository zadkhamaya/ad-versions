One brief in. 18 ad versions out.

I built a workflow that takes one brief and one video clip and returns 18 ad versions, checked against a health brand's advertising rules, approved by a person and packaged for upload.

The brief

A short n8n form. It asks for the brand, product, audience, offer, three key messages and the required disclaimer. It takes the source clip (an upload, or a link for big files) and a shot list in plain words: one line per shot with its time range, what it does in the ad and, where needed, where to crop. Optional fields set the look, landing URL, call to action, and whether voice and b-roll are stand-ins or real.

The workflow

  1. Scripts: two hooks, with a voice-over line for each length.
  2. Compliance: a word list and a second model pass; a failing line is rewritten once, then dropped.
  3. B-roll: shots generated on a GPU pod on demand, then checked.
  4. Voice-over and music: a synthetic voice, an instrumental bed.
  5. Cut and reframe: one edit plan, three crops.
  6. QA on every file.
  7. Contact sheet of all versions.
  8. Approval on Telegram.
  9. Package: renamed files, manifest, report.

The 18 versions

Two hooks, three lengths (6, 15, 30 s), three formats (9:16, 1:1, 16:9). Every file is named by one convention, such as RESTFIELD_ONLINESLEEPCLINIC_H1_15S_9X16_EN_V01.mp4.

Captions are burned into the picture; the voice-over text is in the manifest. The voice is synthetic.

QA and guardrails

Each version gets passed or failed with reasons. A failed render never stops the others. Four sources feed the verdict:

  • the render log;
  • a format and loudness check: exact length and size, 24 fps, H.264 and AAC, −14 LUFS, true peak under −1 dBTP;
  • a transcript of the finished audio, compared with the approved script (word error rate at most 0.15) and scanned for banned words;
  • the end card: a vision model reads the disclaimer back and it must match the approved text.

The rulebook

The rules are written for Australian advertising of a telehealth service. Prescription medicines cannot be advertised to the public, and the TGA counts indirect pointers, including visual cues.

Banned words and claims
  • cure, treat, treatment
  • guaranteed, clinically proven, doctor recommended
  • before and after
  • medication, prescription, melatonin, sleeping pills
  • prices, offers, urgency
Banned in the picture
  • pills, packets, bottles, injection pens, pharmacy bags
  • white coats or stethoscopes as endorsement
  • a phone screen showing a treatment order
  • a person as reviewer, patient or clinician
  • anything implying a medical result

What QA stopped in this run

Two of the 18 failed, both 30 s square versions. QA measures how far each b-roll clip's colour sits from the source footage after grading, and in each of the two one clip came out at 13.5 against a limit of 12. The contact sheet marked them FAIL and they stayed out of the package.

All 12 script lines passed compliance on the first pass, so nothing was rewritten this time. The rewrite path (one rewrite, then the line is dropped) is covered by the workflow's tests.

How it is built, and what is a stand-in

n8n runs the flow. A Python and ffmpeg media service, in Docker next to it on a two-core VPS, renders and checks. Models read scripts, judge compliance and read the end card. Video generation runs on a GPU pod on demand and is deleted after the run. Whisper runs on the CPU.

Synthetic media, disclosed

  • The person in the source clip is AI-generated, used as lifestyle talent only: never speaking to camera, never a patient or clinician.
  • The voice is designed from a text description. No real person is cloned.
  • The music was generated in-house, an instrumental at 72 bpm.
  • The b-roll is generated live by a text-to-video model, with no people in it.
  • Every version is marked synthetic_media: yes in the manifest.

Not production

  • Restfield is a made-up brand. A real brand brings its own approved disclaimer and legal sign-off.
  • The package stands in for platform upload. Production would push to the ad platforms' APIs.
  • The clip stands in for a real shoot. A real clip goes through the same upload path.
